PHASE 01 · COMPLETE · FOUNDATIONAL CONTRACT

Forge an Outlier, Not a Qualified Applicant.

The purpose of this roadmap is not to make a follower "eligible" for an AI job. The purpose is to systematically build an individual whose technical depth, research ability, systems competence, scientific judgment, originality and evidence are strong enough that serious research teams have a reason to notice them. The roadmap therefore trains above the minimum common hiring bar and uses frontier organizations as the calibration point.

01 · CAPABILITYDeep technical commandUnderstand the mathematics, algorithms, models and systems—not just the vocabulary.
02 · RESEARCHIndependent investigationMove from paper → reproduction → experiment → failure analysis → hypothesis → contribution.
03 · EVIDENCEProof, not claimsCode, benchmarks, papers, experiments, talks and external validation demonstrate capability.
04 · RECRUITMENTLab-level fitMap proven capability to specific teams, roles and current hiring requirements.

The New Mastery Standard
KNOW

Explain concepts, mathematics, assumptions, tradeoffs and failure modes without hand-waving.

BUILD

Implement important methods and systems yourself, including from-scratch work where appropriate.

DEBUG / OPTIMIZE

Diagnose failures, profile bottlenecks and improve performance with evidence rather than guessing.

RESEARCH

Generate hypotheses, design experiments, interpret negative results and produce new knowledge.

Evidence Gates
01
Capability Gate

Can the candidate solve and implement the underlying problem without relying on tutorials or AI-generated answers?

02
Reproduction Gate

Can an unfamiliar paper be converted into a working, reproducible experiment with a documented gap analysis?

03
Research Gate

Can the candidate formulate and test an original hypothesis with controls, ablations and rigorous interpretation?

04
Defense Gate

Can the candidate defend their implementation, research decisions, failures and conclusions under hostile technical questioning?

Explicitly NOT the target standard
  • Certificates as proof of mastery.
  • Large numbers of shallow repositories.
  • Follower counts, contribution streaks or arbitrary GitHub stars as readiness gates.
  • Knowing a technology name without being able to implement, debug or reason about it.
  • Completing the calendar and declaring yourself "ready."

Universal Evidence Architecture
RESEARCH OUTPUT

Publications, preprints, reproducible studies, strong research reports or equivalent original work.

RESEARCH ENGINEERING

Ability to implement, scale, debug and extend models/systems instead of merely using libraries.

EXPERIMENTAL RIGOR

Baselines, controls, ablations, measurement, negative-result analysis and defensible conclusions.

SYSTEMS DEPTH

Distributed training, inference, GPU/performance or infrastructure depth where the target team requires it.

ORIGINALITY

Ability to formulate new questions and propose experiments without a pre-written recipe.

COMMUNICATION

Explain technical decisions, research tradeoffs and failures clearly under questioning.

Research Protocol — Every Serious Project
PRE-REGISTRATION MINDSET

Before running the main experiment, write:

  • research question
  • hypothesis
  • expected direction
  • primary metric
  • baseline
  • falsification condition
BASELINE DISCIPLINE

Never compare a new method only against a weak or convenient baseline. Establish the strongest fair reference you can reproduce.

ABLATION DISCIPLINE

Every claimed component needs evidence that it contributes. Remove, replace or perturb components and explain the result.

FAILURE DISCIPLINE

Maintain a failure log. Record what failed, why you think it failed, what changed and what evidence supports the diagnosis.

REPRODUCIBILITY DISCIPLINE

Another researcher should be able to recreate the important result from documented code, environment, data and configuration.

CLAIM DISCIPLINE

Never make a stronger claim than the experiment supports. Separate observation, interpretation, hypothesis and speculation.

Candidate Standards
What these labs actually require — no sugarcoating

Tier-0 Research Track OpenAI · DeepMind · Anthropic · Meta FAIR

Publication at NeurIPS / ICML / ICLR / ACL — or exceptional arXiv output with citations. Exceptional projects can substitute, but it's rare at this tier.
Deep field reading — can discuss 50+ papers fluently and identify gaps in SOTA. Expected to articulate research directions unprompted.
Strong implementation ability — can implement any architecture from scratch. PyTorch internals, custom CUDA, distributed training knowledge.
Original research insight — interviewers probe whether you think like a researcher: "What's the most interesting thing you found that didn't make it into the paper?"

Tier-0 Engineering Track Google Brain · OpenAI · Meta

LeetCode hard — 2–4 algorithmic problems per round. Expect graph traversal, DP, system design with ML components.
ML system design — "Design a ranking system for 1B users" or "Design a distributed training pipeline for a 100B parameter model."
Distributed training + infra knowledge — data parallelism, model parallelism, ZeRO, gradient checkpointing all fair game.
2–3 flagship projects — with real experiments, ablations, and results tables. Clear GitHub history — reviewers check commit quality.

Tier-1 Labs NVIDIA · Apple · Microsoft · Amazon · Tesla

Domain depth over breadth — be the world's expert on something specific. Efficient ML for Apple; CUDA + kernel writing for NVIDIA; real-world CV for Tesla.
Demonstrated impact with numbers — "Reduced latency by 40% while maintaining 98% of full-precision accuracy" beats any vague description.
Public output — GitHub, papers, blog posts, HF Hub models. Reviewers Google you before the first call. Make sure they find something impressive.
Systems thinking — can you deploy your model? Scale it? Debug production failures? This matters more at Tier-1 than pure academic metrics.

Your Medical AI Niche DeepMind Health · Google Health · NVIDIA Clara · Apple Health

Dataset fluency — MIMIC-CXR, CheXpert, BraTS, KITS, CholecT50. Know their biases, splits, and evaluation protocols cold.
Clinical context — understand what a radiologist or clinician actually needs. "Clinically meaningful false positive rate" matters more than raw accuracy.
Regulatory awareness — FDA Class II / III medical device software, CE marking, and what it means for model deployment. Not deep expertise — just awareness.
Responsible AI framing — bias in medical AI, dataset shift across hospitals, demographic fairness in diagnoses. These come up in every medical AI interview.

GitHub Portfolio — Universal Bar

Minimum 3 flagship projects — not tutorials. Each with: clear README, architecture diagram, experiments section, results table, demo GIF.
Consistent commit history — 6+ months of green contribution graph. Discipline is visible from the outside.
At least 1 repo with 50+ stars — external validation that your work is useful to others, not just yourself.
Production code quality — type hints, docstrings, unit tests, logging. Notebook-quality code in production repos is an instant red flag.

Red Flags That Kill Applications

Tutorial projects only — "built a chatbot using LangChain" or "MNIST classifier in PyTorch." Everyone has done this. Zero signal.
Vague GitHub — repos with no README, no experiments, no results. A sparse public GitHub is worse than appearing to have no GitHub at all.
No depth signal — skimming papers without implementing them or finding anything interesting. Labs want researchers, not consumers of research.
Generic cover letters — "I am passionate about AI" with no reference to the lab's specific papers or research direction. Screened out in 10 seconds.
